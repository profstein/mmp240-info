More info on Brainstorming

If the documents above are confusing or you just want more info on the Brainstorming process, read below.

1. Choose Your Topic

Pick one of the approved project types:

  • Travel / Local Guide
  • Hobby Site
  • How-To Guide

Choose something you enjoy, know about, and can support with real content (not AI-generated).

2. Gather Inspiration

Browse websites connected to your topic. Save URLs for:

  • Layouts or designs you like
  • Color ideas
  • Typography styles
  • UI patterns or page structures

You don't need to build anything yet — just explore and collect.

3. Identify Legal Content Sources

Find possible image, video, and text sources you can use. Examples include:

  • Unsplash, Pexels, Pixabay, Wikimedia Commons
  • YouTube or Vimeo (check that the Vimeo video allows embedding)
  • Wikipedia, or other Creative Commons text sources

You must cite every source you use on your website. Keep track of URLs.

4. Record Everything in the Brainstorming Document

Use the Google Doc/MS Word template to store:

  • Your topic choice
  • Inspiration links
  • Possible content sources
  • Notes on early design ideas

Project 4 is Coming Up: Start Thinking About Your Showcase Site

We’ll be kicking off Project 4: My Showcase Site soon. This will be your chance to pull together everything you’ve learned this semester into a polished, multi-page website you can actually show off.

For this project, you’ll choose one of these overall directions:

  • Travel or Local Guide — a place(s) you know well and can make a guide for: “My New York,” “Guide to My Neighborhood,” “Hidden Spots in Queens” etc.
  • Hobby Site — something you care about (and can easily get media for): gaming, baking, skating, music gear, etc.
  • How-To Guide — teaching something you know how to do, and either have images of or can lean on existing media from places like Pexels and YouTube.

The focus of this project is on web design, layout, responsiveness, and interaction — not on writing tons of original content, taking pictures or shooting video.

As you're thinking about your topic, also do some research to see how easy it will be to get content for it. You should pick a topic where you can reuse or curate content (text, images, video) rather than spending all your time creating it from scratch.

Here are some examples of sites where you can reuse content, with citations:

  • YouTube
  • Pexels (there are also videos now)
  • Unsplash
  • Wikipedia (share-alike licensing means you need to also license your site under a CC Share-Alike license.)

You can't just use any content you want, even if you cite it. The author must have explicitly licensed the content, or put it on a platform like YouTube that allows them to choose if it is shared or not.

Planning Documents

We’re going to begin this project with planning, not coding.

Our first step will be a Project Brief document where you’ll outline:

  • Project type (Travel/Local Guide, Hobby, or How-To)
  • Target audience (Who is this site really for? Be specific.)
  • Style direction
    • Mood / vibe (e.g., playful, calm, bold, minimal)
    • Color direction (initial palette ideas)
    • Type direction (what kind of fonts/typography feel right)
  • How you’ll get your content (What content will you write or make? What will come from other sources like Pexels, YouTube, etc.?)
  • Inspiration sites — A short list of example sites (3–5) that you draw ideas and inspiration from — layout, navigation, color, typography, or interaction.

More on this later in the week, but it’s a good time to start thinking about which topic you might choose and what kind of site you’d be excited to design.
